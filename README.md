# CLT Field Simulator

A local Three.js simulator for a fictional Charlotte Magnetic Field.

## Run

```sh
python3 -m http.server 5173
```

Then open:

```text
http://127.0.0.1:5173/
```

The app supports CLT intensity and an automatically calculated M-band radius, true rpm rotation, polarity flips per minute, magnetic axial tilt, colored CLT radius bands, live microtesla output, and tungsten concentration.

The M-band radius follows `M = 1.09 + 40.70 / (1 + (5142 / CLT)^1.542)` in meters. Changing CLT updates the radius, all field bands, electron population, compasses, camera framing and distance readouts. The radius display is read-only and rounded to two decimals; geometry uses the unrounded result. At zero CLT, the formula’s limit is 1.09 m. At the default 1,000 CLT, M is approximately 4.10703 m; at 5,142 CLT, it is 21.44 m.

Universal axial tilt (-180° to 180°) tilts all field bands together around the heart while the human stays upright. It starts at 0° and combines with magnetic axial tilt, rotation, and polarity flips. **Field jitter** (0° to 15°) adds bounded, smooth irregular angular shaking around each source’s heart anchor; zero disables it. Particles and compasses sample the shaken orientation, and the plasma spin axis follows it. The human and recorded trails remain fixed. Reset restores tilt, jitter and capture attraction defaults. Both female models retain the shaped clothing and articulated limbs, with a continuous sculpted face, smaller defined eyes, a swept hairline and layered strands, clear lenses with thin rims and temple arms, and a small marker at the shared heart source. Faces point toward +Z; magnetic transforms never turn the humans.

Enable **Show free electrons** for 1,600 particles by default, adjustable from 100 to 5,000. Initial particles are spread evenly among seven thick CE–MH rings, with independent random azimuths, thickness offsets and small pitch variations. Their azimuthal launch velocity uses the slow circular branch balancing electric, magnetic and centrifugal forces in that source’s current strength, orientation and rotating flow. The speed control perturbs this initial balance. Band choice is an initial condition, not a constraint: later motion can cross bands, transfer or escape. Cyan particles are free, green particles are interacting, gold particles are captured, and white particles are escaping. Initial-population launch speed is adjustable independently of replacement speed.

Escaped particles are replaced one-for-one **around either magnetic pole**, at 1.08 × the 0.72 m source radius (0.7776 m from the heart). Both polar caps are sampled evenly. Their footpoints span multiple dipole flux loops using L = r / sin²(theta), rather than crowding one narrow axial beam. Caps stay within 45° of either pole and follow that source’s tilt, jitter and polarity. Outward, field-aligned launches include random azimuthal handedness and pitch, at **2 × M meters per second** from the full-precision formula (8.21406 m/s at 1,000 CLT). A particle crossing MH continues moving under the fields and can curve back and be recaptured, keeping its identity and trail. Replacement occurs beyond the union of both 4 × MH boundaries or after 20 simulation seconds continuously outside all MH volumes. Returning to either field resets the outside timer; pausing freezes it. Replacements use the last dominant source’s poles and speed and receive a new ID.

Each source uses a **closed dipole field** for electron forces, visible magnetic loops and compass needles. The source is a smooth finite current distribution of radius 0.72 m, centered on the anatomical heart of the upright 1.68 m female model at world position (0.055, 1.24, 0.045) m. Outside it, the dipole field falls as inverse distance cubed. Inside, a finite distributed current produces curved return paths. The source is defined by `A = f(r) (moment × r)`, with `f = (35/8 - 21u/4 + 15u²/8) / a³`, `u = r²/a²`, and `a = 0.72 m`; the field is the curl of A. All components of B and their first derivatives match the external dipole at the source boundary, with no uniform-field direction jump. The field remains divergence-free. Exterior lines obey `r = L sin²(theta)` from northern to southern source footprints and close through return paths traced from the same magnetic vector field using RK4. Rendering reserves 80 of 320 tube segments for the source return at every radius, avoiding the previous straight interior connectors, spline corners and under-resolved center. Small display bands inside the source share the innermost external loops; band readouts still classify actual perpendicular distance from the magnetic axis. Spherical field halos and decorative dust are hidden. Polar plume/axis-beam geometry stays removed.

**Every electron follows integrated local forces** rather than snapping onto a guide, moving to a pole or selecting a scripted orbit. A locally exact helical push advances both position and gyro phase between symmetric electric kicks and preserves magnetic-only speed. This eliminates the standard Boris rotation-angle and straight-chord error when central gyromotion is faster than the step budget. Each step predicts a helical midpoint before resampling magnetic and electric fields. Integration adapts to cyclotron rotation and spatial travel, capped at 64 substeps per 120 Hz physics tick for interactive performance. Exact local gyromotion does not remove spatial-gradient or time-sampling error in nonuniform fields. Helical motion, mirror reversals, curved drift and band crossings follow from the sampled field and velocities. Incoming and escaping particles use the same solver. Field rotation and tilt change the forces; they do not rotate particle positions or recorded histories.

The separate CLT electric model includes a stronger softened radial trapping potential extending beyond MH, smooth ring potential wells around the CE/E/M/PS/MS/MP/MH bands, local softened wells at both poles, and an assumed rotating plasma flow with differential rotation. **Capture attraction** defaults to 2× and ranges from 0× to 5×. It scales the trapping, band and pole forces continuously without recreating the population or its histories; zero disables those forces while the rotating flow remains. Ring wells can attract from either side of nearby bands, and pole wells pull toward either pole without directing particles to prescribed endpoints. The motional electric term `E = -u × B` is integrated as exact local gyromotion relative to that flow. These electric terms can do work; magnetic force alone does not supply attraction or energy. Weak stochastic pitch scattering rotates velocity without adding speed. The electric response uses I/(I + 0.003), where I = CLT/1000, so low-strength sources retain attraction while zero intensity still disables it. A broad softened potential of range 13 × M complements the central, band and pole wells. Rotational plasma entrainment saturates smoothly at 1.25 × sqrt(I/(I + 0.003)) radians per second rather than forcing unbounded corotation; overlap stirring uses the same bounded rates. Modeled plasma relaxation removes excess kinetic energy above 60% of the radial binding depth using an exponential update. It preserves azimuthal momentum up to 4 × M m/s, with a small residual-energy floor, so cooling does not drain circulating rings. Excess tangential speed above this bound can still relax. It vanishes with zero capture attraction and is omitted from magnetic-only reference runs. Capture status includes either small Larmor-radius magnetization or kinetic energy below the radial barrier to MH; it does not change positions. These terms improve retention without forcing every electron to remain trapped. Their coefficients and source strength are model assumptions for the fictional CLT field, not a self-consistent plasma or Maxwell solver. The adaptive step cap and display-unit charge-to-mass ratio limit fidelity at extreme fields. Live readouts distinguish captured/interacting particles, real parallel-velocity reversals, band crossings and actual escape directions; escape paths are not assigned an arbitrary pole/random probability.

Trails stay **off by default**. When enabled, they retain actual world positions and original selected-mode colors at 60 Hz over 0.65 seconds, with only age fading. Rotation, tilt and changes in particle state never move old samples. Physics ticks interpolate field orientation between display frames. Replacing a particle resets that particle’s trail; fresh injection, reset, or a change of radius/count/initial speed starts a new population and history.

Enable **Show compasses** for 18 fixed probes sampling exactly the same vector field as electrons. Red tips point along B and disappear at zero CLT. **Show field lines** controls closed loops and direction tracers independently of physics. Zero CLT disables magnetic and electric forces, leaving ballistic motion. Universal tilt and polarity reversal rotate the field while the human stays upright. Reset restores the formula-derived radius, 1,600 electrons and trails off.

Enable **Second Charlotte → Secondary field** to add another upright model with a heart-centered source. Its CLT sets its own full-precision M radius. Set relative X/Y/Z position, distance, rotation speed, axial tilt, reversed polarity, automatic flip rate and manual flips, and independent field jitter. Secondary microtesla, tungsten, MH radius, viewer band/local CLT and re-entry readouts use its own source parameters. The legend shows both sources’ band radii. Distance changes preserve the current placement direction; universal tilt and jitter act around each heart without tilting either human. The camera frames both sources. Placement and secondary strength edits preserve the current particle population and recorded histories.

Particles and compasses respond to the **vector sum of both B fields** and the sum of their electric forces. In two-source mode, visible guides are RK4 streamlines of that summed field in world space rather than two independent dipole drawings. Each source seeds all eight band levels (NS, CE, E, M, PS, MS, MP, MH), for 64 guides. NS/CE/E guides use brighter red/orange/yellow and draw through the models so the inner field is visible; the guide colors identify their seed bands, and their shapes still follow the combined field. Capture ownership follows the source with the stronger local field, with 12% hysteresis to prevent rapid switching on equal-strength boundaries. Either source can capture particles; particles can transfer as local dominance changes, and stronger sources generally influence a larger region. Actual per-source capture counts are displayed without enforced quotas. Escape replacement uses the outer-boundary/continuous-outside-time rule and the last dominant source’s poles, with 2 × M m/s launch speed. Fresh injection seeds both sources equally. Zeroing one source leaves the other active; zeroing both removes all forces.

**Interaction stirring** (0–3×) adds an explicit rotating plasma vortex where both fields overlap, with entrainment toward the stronger magnetic moment. It fades with distance, scales with rotation, and vanishes when either source is off or both stop rotating. Motional electric fields are summed as `E = -u₁ × B₁ - u₂ × B₂ - u_stir × (B₁ + B₂)`, preserving vector cancellation and avoiding spurious cross terms from summing flow velocities. The combined electric field is decomposed into perpendicular drift and parallel acceleration for the helical integrator. Magnetic-only superposition is physical; the overlap flow and CLT trapping remain illustrative model assumptions.

**Rotation inflow** (0–3×, default 1×) adds a modeled inward plasma-flow component to each rotating source. Its rate scales with absolute RPM and field strength, is softened near the heart, and fades in the outer bands. When capture attraction is active, transport settles toward nearby flux bands, with smooth transitions between their basins and outer decay. The flux label extends continuously through the finite current source using its vector potential, so a polar leg is recognized as part of its whole loop instead of automatically being treated as CE. With attraction off, transport stays inward. Zero disables this added transport; a stationary or zero-intensity source contributes none. It enters through the same motional electric field as orbital flow without changing positions directly. Rotation alone does not imply universal inward drift; unmagnetized particles and parallel motion need not follow the inward flow. Both sources contribute their own inflow before electric-field superposition.

Click or tap an individual visible electron to open its **live inspector**: ID, status, dominant Charlotte, band, distance from that heart, speed, local combined magnetic field, bounces, band changes, transfers and age. A ring tracks its actual world position. Camera drags do not select particles, and the capture filter hides the ring with its particle. Replacement electrons get new IDs; an escaped selection ends with “Escaped · replaced” rather than following a different electron. Restarting a population ends the old selection. Close the panel or press Escape to clear it.

**Hide uncaptured electrons** renders only particles currently captured (magnetized or bound by the CLT trap), while all electrons continue moving, entering, escaping, and contributing to readouts. **Electron colors** selects status, a single picked color, distance from the dominant source’s heart, or instantaneous speed. Distance runs from warm near the heart to violet at/beyond MH. Speed runs from blue to red, normalized to the source radius and launch-speed setting; the gradient saturates above that scale. Live color changes do not recolor old trail samples.

The top-bar **Pause/Resume** control freezes simulation time, field rotation, jitter, flips and particle motion while camera controls remain usable. **Advance one frame** moves time by 1/60 second while paused. **Stable path view** copies the actual recent trail geometry and recorded colors into a fixed snapshot, hiding live trails while it is enabled. **Refresh frozen paths** takes a new snapshot. Capture filtering of frozen paths uses capture status at snapshot time; frozen geometry and original colors remain unchanged by later motion or color edits. Reset restores running time and default display controls.

Two-source verification also covers magnetic cancellation, electric-field decomposition, local dominance, capture in either source, overlap stirring limits, no ownership teleportation, and zero-field ballistic motion.

Rotation-inflow verification covers source/RPM/gain scaling, central regularity, outer decay, integrated inward band transport, polarity reversal, high-field stability, and electric superposition through both samplers. Browser verification covers the inspector lifecycle, exterior timeout renewal, secondary flip and jitter parity, all eight dual-field guides, clear glasses, face/hair rendering and unchanged body profiles.

The clear lenses use thin alpha-blended material instead of full-scene transmission buffers. Rendering caps the drawing buffer at two million pixels and 1.5× device scaling, without retaining the drawing buffer between frames. A lost graphics context pauses simulation time and shows a recovery message with a reload action. When the browser restores the context, rendering resumes at no more than 1× scaling and discards elapsed recovery time. Module/startup failures also show a reload action instead of leaving an unexplained blank viewer. Browser checks cover high-density screens, actual context loss/restoration, and the character, field and particle views after recovery.

Weak-field retention verification includes 1–1,000 CLT with a tilted field at 350 RPM, finite energy, magnetic-only conservation, gradual exterior attraction, bounded entrainment, smooth excess-energy relaxation, and recapture after returning across MH. Browser verification checks outside grace periods, pole replacements and source ownership, unchanged particle IDs on return, balanced counts, live capture filtering and pause behavior.

Ring-distribution regressions verify strength-specific circular launch balance, polar injection across flux shells, smooth band transport, preservation of bounded orbital momentum during cooling, and sustained multi-band circulation for resident and replacement ensembles over a minute without recycling.
